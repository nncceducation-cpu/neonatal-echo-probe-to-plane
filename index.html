What it is for

    Move the probe

    Rotate
    Sweep (new plane)
    Rock (same plane)
    Slide
     

    Rotating about the beam changes which section you get. Sweeping tilts the beam about the index mark, which moves the plane itself and is how one view becomes another. Rocking stays inside the current plane, so the cross-section does not change at all — only the part of it the sector covers. Sliding changes the window. Watch the plane angle below: that is how far off the view you are.

    Sources, conventions and limitations

    Index-mark directions are given in plain anatomical terms rather than as clock positions, because a clock position needs a 12 o’clock reference on the probe face and that reference is ambiguous in the suprasternal notch, where the face lies almost horizontal. Where the source material states a pointer position, it is quoted and attributed on the view itself.

    The registered heart mesh and chamber labels derive from BodyParts3D (DBCLS, CC BY-SA 2.1 Japan), scaled into a term-newborn patient frame. Both cut panels use the same section geometry and leaflet intersections. The atlas and reconstructed valve surfaces are teaching approximations; correspondence with supplied SCAN references needs clinical review. Diastolic valve profiles are static; they are not synchronized to the recorded clips. Clinical review is pending. This is not patient-derived neonatal imaging or a realistic ultrasound simulator. The probe-pose rules are checked by tools/verify_planes.py, which runs this page’s own geom.js and slicer.js.

    A teaching reconstruction, not a diagnostic tool. The recorded clips are independent clinical studies, not spatially or temporally registered to this model.
